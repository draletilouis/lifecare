Before your visit

You do not need an appointment for the outpatient clinic or Accident & Emergency. If you would like a set time, request an appointment and we will call you to confirm.

What to bring

Tick each item as you pack it, or send the list to the person bringing you.

Send this list on WhatsApp

Registering

On your first visit, reception will record your details and give you a patient number. Keep it safe: it lets us find your records quickly on every later visit.

Returning patients only need to give their name or patient number.

Languages

Our team speaks English, Lugbara and Swahili. If you need help in another language, tell reception and we will do our best to find someone to help, or bring a relative who can interpret.

Referred to us?

If another health facility has referred you, bring the referral letter and any results. If you need care we do not provide, we will refer you on with a letter summarising your care.

Coming from outside Uganda?

Patients travel to us from South Sudan, the DR Congo and further away. Read our guide for travelling patients and message us before you set off.

Health checks

Check-ups for adults, mothers and children, with a clear list of what each includes. See our health check packages.

Paying for your care

We accept Cash, MTN Mobile Money, Airtel Money and approved insurance or employer schemes. Always ask for a receipt, and ask for an itemised bill if you would like to see each charge.

Paying yourself

Pay at the cashier in cash, or by MTN Mobile Money or Airtel Money. For tests, scans or a planned operation, ask for an estimate first so you know what to expect.

Insurance and employer schemes

Bring your membership card or employer letter on every visit. Our billing team will confirm your cover. Admissions and operations may need approval from your insurer first, and we will help you request it.

Items your cover may not include

Some schemes do not cover every medicine, test or service, or ask you to pay part of the cost. Our billing team will tell you before you are charged.

Staying in hospital

Patients are admitted from Accident & Emergency, the clinics, maternity or after an operation. Nurses care for you day and night, and a doctor reviews you on a ward round every day.

What to bring for a stay

  • ID, and your insurance card or approval
  • All the medicines you take
  • Nightwear, a change of clothes, towel and slippers
  • Soap, toothbrush and other toiletries
  • A phone and charger

Attendants

A relative or friend can usually stay with children, mothers and patients who need extra help. Ask the nurse in charge about the arrangements on your ward.

Keeping informed

Ask the nurse in charge at any time about your treatment plan, your progress and your bill. Valuables are your responsibility, so please leave them at home where you can.

Going home

Before you leave, you will receive your medicines with instructions, a discharge summary and a date for any follow-up visit. Your bill is settled at the cashier or with your insurer.

Visiting hours and rules

Visitors help patients recover. To let patients rest and the team work, please visit during these times. Some wards have their own arrangements; check with the nurse in charge before you travel.

Visiting arrangements by ward
WardVisiting timesWho can visit
General wards12:30 pm – 2:00 pm and 4:30 pm – 7:00 pmTwo visitors at a bedside
Maternity ward12:30 pm – 2:00 pm and 4:30 pm – 7:00 pmThe partner and one other adult

Newborn careParents at any timeNo other visitors, to protect babies from infection

Please

  • Keep to two visitors at a bedside at a time
  • Clean your hands when you arrive on the ward and when you leave
  • Speak quietly and switch your phone to silent
  • Follow the nurses’ advice; they may limit visits when a patient needs rest

Protecting patients

  • Do not visit if you have a fever, cough, diarrhoea or vomiting
  • Bring young children only after checking with the nurse in charge
  • Check with the nurse before bringing food for a patient
  • Do not take photos of other patients

Your rights and responsibilities

We follow the principles of the Ministry of Health’s Patients’ Charter. Good care is a partnership between you and the people looking after you.

As our patient, you have the right to

  • Be treated with respect, dignity and courtesy, without discrimination
  • Receive emergency care when you need it
  • Clear information about your condition, treatment, risks and costs
  • Give or refuse consent to treatment, and ask for a second opinion
  • Privacy during examination, and confidentiality of your information
  • Know the name and role of the people treating you
  • Complain and have your complaint looked into

We ask you to

  • Give accurate information about your health, medicines and past treatment
  • Follow the treatment you agree to, and tell us if you cannot
  • Treat staff and other patients with respect
  • Keep appointments, or let us know if you cannot come
  • Settle your bill, or agree a payment plan with billing
  • Respect hospital property and the privacy of others

Medical records and privacy

Your medical records are confidential. Only the people involved in your care can see them, and we share them with others, such as your insurer or another hospital, only with your consent or where the law requires it.

You can ask for a copy of your results, a medical report or your discharge summary at reception. Some reports may take a few days and may carry a fee. Read our privacy notice to learn how we handle information sent through this website.

Feedback and complaints

We want to hear what we do well and where we can do better. Compliments help us thank the people who cared for you; complaints help us put things right.

  1. Speak to us on the spot. Most concerns can be solved straight away by the nurse in charge or the head of the department.
  2. Ask for the hospital administrator if you are not satisfied, or contact us by email at info@lifecare.example or on WhatsApp.
  3. We will listen, look into it and reply. Your care will not be affected in any way by a complaint.

If you are still not satisfied, you may raise your concern with the relevant professional council in Uganda, such as the Uganda Medical and Dental Practitioners Council.
