# What Africa's leading hospital websites do, and what Life Care takes from them

Studied October 2026. Desktop (1440 px) and phone (390 px) captures were taken in a real browser, and each page's fonts, colours, navigation, section order, contact links, structured data and page weight were measured.

## The hospitals

There is no official pan-African hospital ranking. We used the composite top 10 published by African Exponent (October 2026). Its first five places come from Brand Finance's Global Top 250 Hospitals 2026; places six to ten come from international accreditation and referral roles.

## What the best of them do

1. **Keep emergency contact visible on every page.** Examples: the Netcare 911 utility bar, Korle Bu's ticker, Evercare's full-width red "For emergencies, call…" bar, and the emergency number in the Nairobi Hospital's top bar.
2. **Lead with patient tasks rather than the institution.** Evercare has six tiles (appointment, teleconsult, refer a patient, offers, second opinion, home care). Netcare and Mediclinic pair a search box with task chips.
3. **Help other clinicians refer patients.** Evercare asks "You are referring a patient as a… hospital / doctor", then sets out a three-step process. Aga Khan has a Patient Referral Office.
4. **Look after travelling and cross-border patients.** Aga Khan and King Faisal offer visa help, interpreters in named languages, insurance coordination and a dedicated desk.
5. **Publish ward-by-ward visiting rules.** The Nairobi Hospital shows a table by ward (General, ICU, Maternity, Paediatrics) with notes such as "Max 2 visitors" or "One parent overnight", plus visitor guidelines. Groote Schuur does the same.
6. **Be open about prices.** Aga Khan publishes priced screening packages, Life Healthcare and Mediclinic have fixed-fee procedures, and Evercare has package tabs.
7. **Publish health information and alerts.** Aga Khan ran an Ebola "know the signs" alert. Korle Bu has "When to take your child to emergency". Lagos University Teaching Hospital and Mediclinic run health libraries.
8. **Show proof of credibility.** Accreditation logos (Aga Khan), stats (Korle Bu, Evercare), credential chips (Korle Bu), patient stories (Aga Khan, Korle Bu), and doctor directories with photo, specialty, biography and booking (Nairobi Hospital).
9. **Use local languages.** Evercare offers patient rights in English, Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and Hindi. Aga Khan lists its interpreter languages.
10. **Provide practical tools.** Korle Bu has an interactive visit checklist. Lagos University Teaching Hospital publishes a clinic-days timetable.

## Where they fall short, and where Life Care can do better

- **Speed:** homepages weigh 0.8 to 13.5 MB and take 3 to 36 s to load. Life Care stays under 1 MB.
- **Hidden content:** scroll animations hide content, sometimes permanently (Korle Bu, King Faisal). Life Care uses no hidden-until-scroll content.
- **WhatsApp:** it is rare and usually secondary. In Uganda it is primary.
- **Templates and claims:** generic templates and stock claims ("state-of-the-art", "world-class") with nothing behind them. Life Care states only what the hospital confirms.
- **Mobile money:** none of these sites mentions it.

## What Life Care implements from this

- A "Refer a patient" page for health workers across West Nile.
- A page for patients from South Sudan, the DR Congo and further afield.
- A health information hub with clinically framed articles, each with a pending clinical-review line.
- Health check packages showing what each includes, priced once the hospital confirms prices.
- A visiting-hours table by ward, and visitor guidelines.
- An interactive "What to bring" checklist that can be sent to a relative on WhatsApp.
- A "Save our numbers" contact card for phones.
- A public-health alert band, switched off until it is needed.
- Credential chips under the hero, plus space for stats, accreditations and doctors, filled only from confirmed data.
- On phones, a first screen that shows the headline and main actions.
